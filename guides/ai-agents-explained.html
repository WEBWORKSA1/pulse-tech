AI Agents Explained: What They Are, What They Can Do, and Where They Fail

By Pulse.Tech Editorial · 7 min read · Updated November 2026

A plain-English explainer of AI agents — how they plan, use tools and act — plus realistic use cases and risks for businesses.

"Agent" has become the most overused word in technology. Strip away the marketing and the idea is simple: an AI agent is a language model that can decide on a sequence of actions, use tools to carry them out, look at the results and continue until a goal is met.

Chatbot vs agent

A chatbot answers. An agent acts. Ask a chatbot for a travel plan and it writes one. Ask an agent and it might search flights, compare prices, check your calendar and draft the booking for your approval. The difference is tool access and a loop: plan, act, observe, repeat.

The building blocks

  • Model: the reasoning engine that decides the next step.
  • Tools: APIs, search, code execution, databases, browsers.
  • Memory/context: what the agent knows about the task so far.
  • Guardrails: permissions, budgets, and human approval points.

Where agents work well today

  • Software development tasks with tests to verify results.
  • Research that gathers and summarises many sources.
  • Data cleanup and transformation with clear rules.
  • Customer support triage with escalation to humans.

Where they still fail

Agents struggle with long tasks where a small early mistake compounds, with ambiguous goals, and with actions that cannot be undone. They can also be misled by malicious instructions hidden in content they read — a risk known as prompt injection.

Design principle: give agents narrow permissions, a clear definition of done, and a human checkpoint before anything irreversible.

Getting started

Pick a contained, verifiable task, run the agent in a sandbox, log every action, and measure success rate over at least 50 runs before trusting it with production work.
