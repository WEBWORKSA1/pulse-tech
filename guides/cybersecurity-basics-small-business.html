Cybersecurity Basics for Small Businesses: A 10-Step Baseline

By Pulse.Tech Editorial · 8 min read · Updated November 2026

Ten high-impact, low-cost security controls every small business should have in place, in priority order.

Most breaches that hit small organisations are not sophisticated. They exploit reused passwords, unpatched software and convincing phishing emails. The good news: a small set of controls blocks most of that. Here they are in priority order.

The 10-step baseline

  1. Turn on multi-factor authentication (MFA) everywhere, starting with email, banking, domain registrar and admin accounts. Prefer authenticator apps or passkeys over SMS.
  2. Use a password manager for the whole team so every account has a unique, long password.
  3. Patch automatically. Enable automatic updates on operating systems, browsers and plugins. Old software is the most common open door.
  4. Back up with the 3-2-1 rule: three copies, two media types, one offsite or offline. Test a restore every quarter.
  5. Lock down email with SPF, DKIM and DMARC records so attackers cannot easily spoof your domain.
  6. Limit admin rights. Daily work should happen on standard accounts.
  7. Train for phishing with short, regular refreshers. Make reporting a suspicious email easy and blame-free.
  8. Protect endpoints with built-in or managed endpoint protection and disk encryption on every laptop.
  9. Offboard properly: remove access the same day someone leaves, including shared accounts.
  10. Write a one-page incident plan: who to call, how to isolate a device, where backups live.

Where to spend first

If you can only do three things this week, do MFA, a password manager and verified backups. Together they neutralise account takeover and ransomware — the two incidents most likely to end a small business.

When to bring in help

Consider a managed security partner when you handle payment or health data, have more than 20 devices, face customer security questionnaires, or simply cannot assign an internal owner. A good partner should give you a written baseline assessment before selling you anything.
